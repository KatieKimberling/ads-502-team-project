### **Abstract**

Over a third of Tanzania’s citizens lack proximal access to clean, safe, potable water.
As water is used for the sustenance of life: from drinking to agriculture to hygiene, lack of access poses significant and severe humanitarian, public health, and agricultural threats.
Waterpoints have been funded, constructed and installed through global initiatives, but many lie in extremely remote areas where the waterpoint represents the sole water source for thousands of people, and repair resources (funding and skilled workers) in the event of pump failure are scant.
A reliable and accurate algorithm to predict waterpoint failure in advance allows for targeted deployment of resources in a timely manner to keep the water flowing, saving lives and saving money.
To predict pump functionality, five models: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree (CART), Random Forest, Naive Bayes, and XGBoost were constructed on such attributes as longitude, latitude, year of construction, population served, water quantity, and total static head for the pump.
While Random Forest edged out the other models in terms of accuracy and precision, XGBoost remained the clear winner with the highest AUC ROC.

Keywords: Tanzania, water, XGBoost, Random Forest, data mining

### **Goal**

Develop an algorithm to determine which attributes would predict which WPs are most likely to fail in order for meager resources to be deployed most effectively to keep the water flowing in Tanzania.
Knowing which pumps are likely to fail in advance allows oversight (and funding) agencies to deploy repair measures prior to a complete shutdown or unavailability of water at a source, saving lives and saving money.
